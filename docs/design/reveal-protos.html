Reveal overhaul · spike → ship

Three ways to make a hit land

Today the reveal animates inside a 240 px square that ends at the basepaths. HIT_TARGETS puts a home run at y = 16 and a triple at y = 52 — both crammed just above second base, because there is nowhere else to put them. Three candidate directions, each rendered against the same six-case stress set.

Outcome illegible
A home run, a gapper and a bloop resolve to the same place. Motion carries no information the text doesn't already give.
Space reads wrong
A batted ball leaves or stops at an arbitrary edge of an infield-only frame.

Where each one stands against ADR-0012

Within ADR-0012

A · Full Park

Still chalk lines on navy — it just draws the other 280 feet of them. No illustration, no texture, no rendering. The safest direction to ship and the least surprising to look at.

Costs the most vertical space: the field goes from square to a 440×360 frame, which reshapes the reveal layout on a phone.

Within ADR-0012 · chosen

B · Broadcast

Identical geometry to A — only the viewBox moves. The chalk direction is untouched because nothing new is drawn; the camera is the entire idea. C's distance readout comes along as a broadcast stat bug rather than a dashboard panel.

Chosen because it gets A's spatial fix and C's numeric legibility without paying for either separately: the ball needs somewhere to go, the frame only widens when the play earns it, and the feet state the outcome outright.

Settled: the camera holds the framing the play earned rather than punching back in for the runners, so the resting frame stays a readout of how far the ball went. Playback runs at 1.5× the original beat.

The open question is still reduced motion — strip the camera and it becomes A, which is a perfectly good fallback but should be a deliberate one.

Needs an amendment

C · Telemetry

Leans hardest into "diagram, never illustration" — so hard it stops being a ballpark and becomes an instrument. That's arguably more ADR-0012 than ADR-0012, but it's a different product, so the ADR should say so out loud.

Reads outcomes numerically, which fixes legibility outright — at the risk of trading warmth for a dashboard.

Throwaway prototype code — not production-integrated. Geometry, base-running order, travel durations (0.5 s per base, 0.6 s floor) and the 0.12 s runner stagger are lifted from fieldMovement.ts; per-outcome hold values from scenario.ts. The scoreboard flap pre-roll is elided — the clock here starts at the outcome callout. Pacing is the preserved baseline, not a variable.
